Hi everyone, my name is Quan, a member of AI Resident Batch 4 at [FPT AI Residency](https://wp.fpt-aic.com/ai-residency/). I recently returned from attending the [ICLR 2025](https://iclr.cc/) in Singapore, a trip proudly supported by the FPT AI Residency program. I was accompanied by many fellow AI Residents, and together we had the honor of presenting our research—including 7 papers at the main conference and 5 workshop papers. In this post, I’m excited to share my personal experience from attending the ICLR 2025, along with the valuable insights I gained throughout the event.

The International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR) is one of the most prestigious conferences in the field of Machine Learning, ranked A*. This year’s conference attracted approximately 11,000 attendees. The conference was organized into three main days: the first three days for the main conference and the last two days for workshops. The main conference was incredibly crowded, with researchers from all over the world, many of whom were young like myself. The poster and oral sessions were packed with lively discussions. Researches on Large Language Models (LLMs) and Diffusion Models remain a subject of great interest and attention. Throughout the conference, I gained valuable insights into my favorite topic, Efficient AI. I also observed high community interest in works related to quantization and cost-effective methods for LLMs and Diffusion Models. These works, many of which were led by major industrial research labs like NVIDIA, Google, and Apple, sparked extensive discussion and drew large audiences.

The workshops attracted a large number of attendees, including many professors and senior researchers who organized workshops or attended for community discussions on specific topics. During these workshops, I had the opportunity to meet and interview with several professors who were seeking PhD students. Among the many workshops held at ICLR, the most attended were “World Models", "Reasoning and Planning for LLMs”, “Agentic AI for Science”, and “Sparsity in LLMs.” These workshops reflected the enduring popularity of LLMs and the future research directions in this field. At one of the highest-attending workshops, [Agentic AI for Science](https://iclragenticai.github.io/), our paper titled “Large Language Models Powered Neural Solvers for Generalized Vehicle Routing Problems” was awarded the Second-Best Paper Honourable Mention.

Attending the ICLR conference provided me with valuable opportunities to expand my professional network. I had the privilege of meeting mentors and researchers from renowned Vietnamese institutions, including our esteemed FPT AI Residency, Quancom AI, VinUni, Viettel, and many others. These connections allowed me to gain insights into the vibrant Vietnamese community at a top-tier international conference. Furthermore, I had the chance to make friends with Vietnamese PhD students studying abroad, who shared their experiences and insights into the life and opportunities of PhD students in Singapore and the United States. These interactions broadened my understanding of the challenges and aspirations of PhD students in the field. Additionally, I had the opportunity to meet my collaboraters from The University of Warwick for the first time. We engaged in meaningful discussions about our research topics and future projects. One of the highlights of my conference experience was meeting Professor Yann LeCun, a renowned AI researcher. I was fortunate enough to witness him connect with young and enthusiastic researchers at the conference, which left a lasting impression on me.

In conclusion, attending the ICLR has been a valuable and rare opportunity for me to grow academically, expand my network, and deepen my experience. I would like to express my deepest gratitude to FPT AI Residency for supporting many meaningful conference trips to our AI Resident.
